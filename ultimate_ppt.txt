SLIDE 2 - PROBLEM STATEMENT

Title:
Valid consumer complaints fail before formal redressal

Body:
- Consumers often give up after first-line support fails because they do not know what to say next, where to escalate, or what evidence actually matters.
- Policies, grievance channels, and rights are fragmented across company pages, regulator notices, and support systems.
- Generic chatbots provide advice, but not structured, escalation-ready complaint outputs.
- As a result, a valid grievance rarely becomes a properly documented and actionable case.

High-impact callout:
900,000+ complaints were received by India’s consumer forums between 2020 and 2023.

Key insight line:
The problem is not just complaint filing. The problem is complaint escalation.

What to say:
"The core problem is not that consumers do not have valid complaints. It is that valid complaints collapse after first-line support fails. People do not know what to say next, where to escalate, or which evidence matters. Policies do exist, but they are fragmented and inaccessible at the point of frustration."

Visual idea:
- a 3-part problem graphic:
  - unclear rights
  - fragmented evidence
  - no escalation path

SLIDE 3 - PROPOSED SOLUTION AND KEY FEATURES

Title:
Redressa converts complaint evidence into action-ready escalation outputs

Body:
- Users submit a complaint, merchant details, and supporting evidence.
- The system extracts structured facts and timeline signals from messy inputs.
- It retrieves relevant company policies and regulatory grounding.
- It evaluates whether consumer rights were violated.
- It recommends escalation routes and generates ready-to-use outputs.

Key outputs generated:
- Grievance email
- Escalation note
- Evidence checklist
- Structured case summary

Differentiator:
Not a chatbot. A workflow that prepares the next action.

What to say:
"Redressa closes the gap between complaint and action. The user enters the grievance and uploads evidence. The system extracts facts, retrieves policies and regulations, evaluates the situation, recommends escalation routes, and generates outputs the user can actually send or use."

Visual idea:
- a workflow diagram:
  Intake -> OCR / Parsing -> Fact Extraction -> Policy Retrieval -> Evaluation -> Route Selection -> Output Generation

SLIDE 6 - CHALLENGES FACED + FUTURE SCOPE AND SCALABILITY

Title:
Challenges faced, and how this can scale

Left side - Challenges:
- Designing a system that gives grounded outputs, not generic AI advice
- Preventing unrelated merchant policies from leaking into wrong cases
- Handling messy real-world complaint evidence under OCR limitations
- Keeping the workflow explainable and demoable within hackathon constraints
- Balancing automation with user control in communication and escalation flows

Right side - Future scope:
- Expand policy coverage to more merchants and complaint categories
- Upgrade retrieval using pgvector for semantic policy matching
- Strengthen inbound reply handling and supervised follow-up automation
- Improve deployment hardening and live communication workflows
- Build richer user history and scalable complaint management

Bottom line:
We started narrow with stronger grounded categories instead of pretending universal legal coverage from day one.

What to say:
"One of our biggest challenges was grounding. We did not want a system that sounds intelligent but gives generic advice. So we focused on structured retrieval, evaluation, and outputs. For future scope, this system can expand through broader policy coverage, semantic retrieval using pgvector, stronger communication automation, and richer case continuity."

Visual idea:
- two-column layout:
  Challenges vs Future Scope
- or a Now -> Next -> Scale roadmap
